Is clearing trades as a batch worth it?

Base · 14,490 settled trades over 13.25 hours (2026-08-03, $9.17M) · APEX batch auction over the Fynd solver · companion to the re-solving reports

The question

Fynd quotes every trade alone, against AMM pools. A batch auction can instead collect the trades of a window, cross them against each other where their tokens overlap, route the remainder through pools, and settle everything at one uniform clearing price per pair. This report measures how much output that adds per order, in basis points, for batch windows of 1, 5, 15, 30 and 150 Base blocks (2 s to 5 min).

How it was measured

  1. Take one day of real decoded Base solver trades — every settled swap the hindsight decoder attributes across ~30 venue/solver books, fee-adjusted, wash-filtered (14,490 orders).
  2. Partition each window's trades into connected components (orders sharing tokens, directly or through pools). Components with at least two orders go to the APEX batch solver with the 400 deepest native pools around the order tokens (min TVL 1 ETH); singleton orders are not batchable and score zero.
  3. Every order gets a limit: 100 bps below its baseline. The baseline is the per-order routing quote at the exact same market state the batch clears at (Base block 49,575,430), so the batch and the alternative see identical prices — no time-drift enters the comparison.
  4. An order's gain is max(0, batch output − routing baseline), pro-rata for partial fills, valued in USD at day prices. Orders whose token-pair price moved more than 100 bps between trade day and measurement state are excluded from gain (drift guard).
Same positive-only convention as the re-solving reports. Batching is optional per order (the limit floors it), so gain is never negative for the order that gains — but a uniform clearing price can also fill an order's counterparty below its own baseline. That side is reported separately in the net column rather than hidden.

How the average decomposes

avg gain per unit of flow = (share of flow batched) × (gain on batched flow)

The rightmost columns are what applies to an arbitrary dollar of the day's flow, including the ~85–99% that no batch can touch. As in the re-solving measurement, the growth comes from the frequency side: the share of flow that finds a counterparty grows 116× from w=1 to w=150, while the gain on batched flow stays in a narrow 19–32 bps band. Unlike re-solving, batching has a real negative side: at a uniform clearing price the gaining order's counterparty can do worse than its own routing baseline — at w=5 the counterparty losses fully consume the gains.

Batch clearing loses to per-order routing at every window, by a median of roughly one limit-width at small windows, narrowing as batches grow. The trend is real — more counterparties give the auction better price discovery — but on this flow, at these window lengths, multilateral clearing does not beat good routing on price. The batching case therefore rests on what this report does not price: settlement-gas sharing, MEV protection from uniform clearing, and the no-route long tail that routing cannot serve at all.

Solver budget

APEX searches clearing prices under a deadline. At the production-realistic 1 s budget, 96–99% of pooled component solves return nothing and fall back to order-crossing only; at 4 s, half still do (solve wall times: mean 2.3–2.4 s, p50 3.0–3.3 s, p99 pinned at the deadline). Pool liquidity inside the auction is therefore budget-bound, not economics-bound: where the search does complete (1–5-block windows), pools add 9–69% more matched flow. The numbers above use the 1 s budget; they understate what an auction with a restructured search (or partial-credit at the deadline) could match through pools.

Dataset and limits

  • Survivor flow. These are trades that chose instant aggregator execution and settled. Flow that would opt into a 5-minute batch is a different population; match rates for a deployed auction could differ in either direction.
  • 13.25 h of one day (00:00–13:15 UTC; the US session is absent), one market state, no error bars. The companion re-solving caveat applies: numbers reflect how much this particular day moved.
  • Synthetic limits. Floors are 100 bps below each order's baseline; real user limits were not extracted. A 50/200 bps sweep moved matched volume only slightly.
  • Gross of gas. Neither the batch's settlement-gas sharing (batching's most obvious saving) nor routing's per-trade gas is priced. Latency cost of waiting out the window is not priced either.
  • Pool universe: 400 deepest native pools around the order tokens (of 2,702 candidates ≥ 1 ETH TVL); hooked Uniswap v4 (creator-coin pools) and RFQ liquidity are not simulatable and are absent. The unpriceable long tail — 17% of the day's USD — cannot enter any cell.
  • Drift guard is a bound, not a proof: pair moves under 100 bps between trade day and measurement state pass through into gain.
